01 — Motivation

Why I do this work

Modern AI systems are extraordinarily capable, and extraordinarily opaque. As they find their way into high-stakes domains like medicine, journalism, and environmental monitoring, the gap between what a model does and what we can understand about what it does becomes a fundamental problem.

I'm drawn to questions about how multimodal models can be made more interpretable, efficient, and grounded in physical reality and use the richness of modality for this purpose. Not more powerful in the abstract, but more trustworthy in the specific.

Beyond a standalone research topic, I believe the most interesting problems live at disciplinary boundaries — between computer science and medicine, between perception and language, between the digital and the physical.

I also believe that research has a responsibility to be accessible. I'm drawn to teaching, to writing that reaches non-specialists, and to building tools that make the consequences of AI research legible to the people it affects.

DeLDRify

LDR-to-HDR Image Reconstruction via Generative Adversarial Networks

Standard photographs discard most of the luminance information captured by the human eye. DeLDRify reconstructs this lost dynamic range from a single LDR image using a custom GAN architecture trained to hallucinate physically plausible highlight and shadow detail.

The project included a full ablation study, quantitative comparison with SOTA baselines, and a CVPR-format scientific report covering the theoretical motivation, architecture design, and experimental results.
